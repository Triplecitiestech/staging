This is the field guide for low voltage work with Triple Cities Tech: how tools, the van, materials and devices are handled, what to text and when, and what to do when something goes wrong on a job. Search it or tap a section. The six rules below cover most situations. Everything after them is the specific answer to a specific problem.

  1. Bring your own basic tools. Drill, bits, punch-down, crimper, strippers, tester, hand tools. If it fits in one bag and any low voltage tech owns one, it is yours, not TCT's.
  2. Nothing of TCT's leaves without a text, and every text says the condition. "Taking [tool] – [customer] – works." "Returned [tool] – works." A tool nobody reported broken is charged to the last person who had it.
  3. Every item has a home. Put it there. If the drawer is labeled, the label wins. No label: staging shelf, photo, text. Never the front seat. Never the floor. Never the garbage. If the home does not exist yet, make one and tell Kurtis.
  4. Box, bracket, and device stay together until the device is mounted, powered, and tested. Loose hardware goes in a labeled bag inside the job tote. Cardboard comes back to the garage.
  5. No next job until the last one is emailed. Customer, start, stop, travel, what you did, receipts. To Kurtis, copy Rio. Before you drive to the next site, and always before end of day.
  6. Reset the van, then bring the keys and the card to Kurtis. Quarter tank minimum. Interior photo. Keys and the Amex go to Kurtis's home office every night. The van never goes home with you.

This playbook is the operating manual for anyone doing cabling, camera, access point, rack, and related installation work under Triple Cities Tech. It is written to be searched on a phone in the van and to be printed. When something happens that you do not have an answer for, go to If this, do this and type a word.

When this book is missing something, you own it. If a step calls for a bin that does not exist, a label that is not there, or a situation nobody wrote down: text Kurtis, then solve it yourself the clean way. Make the bin. Write the label. Bag and tag the part. Throwing something in the garbage, or setting it down anywhere, is always the least acceptable answer.

How loss and damage is decided

This is the part that closes the gap in an honor system. You are protected by what you report, and exposed by what you do not.

SituationWho pays
Tool is found broken. Last person who had it texted "Returned – works" and said nothing about damage.That last person.
Tool is found broken. Last person who had it never texted "Returned" at all.That last person. Silence is the same as "I returned it working."
You pick up a tool and it is already broken. You text "Taking – [defect] – photo" before you use it.Not you. Your Taking text moved the cost back to the previous holder.
You pick up a tool, use it all day, and report the defect afterward.You. Check the tool when you take it, not when you return it.
Two people both claim it worked when they had it and nobody has a photo.Split between the two most recent holders.
Tool is missing and the last "Taking" text has no matching "Returned."The person who texted "Taking."
Thirty seconds protects you. Pull the trigger, cycle the chuck, check the battery, glance at the blade. Text what you see. That is the whole inspection.

What TCT will not do

  • Replace your personal tools. If your drill dies, you may use the TCT backup for the rest of that day only, then it goes back with a "Returned" text.
  • Lend TCT tools or the van for non-TCT jobs. First occurrence is removal from the schedule.
  • Buy a tool onsite because one was left behind. Plan your kit before you leave the garage.

Van rules

  • Garage to job to garage. No personal use, no side stops other than fuel or approved materials, no passengers who are not working the job. The van never goes home with a contractor.
  • Keys and the Amex are picked up from and returned to Kurtis's home office every day. Not left in the van, not kept overnight.
  • Start-of-day check, 3 minutes. Photo of the interior and fuel gauge before you touch anything, texted to Kurtis. If the previous user left it wrong, that photo is what keeps it off your record.
  • Fuel. Never return the van below a quarter tank. Fill with the TCT Amex, receipt in the envelope, receipt photo in your job email.
  • Anything wrong with the van, a tool, or materials (warning light, tire, noise, damage, broken gear, bad cable, short count) is photographed and texted to Kurtis and Rio immediately, before you drive further or keep working.
  • Front seat and floor are not storage. An access point on the passenger seat with its bracket in a pile in the back is the exact thing this book exists to stop.
  • End of day: Van reset, interior photo texted, then keys and card to Kurtis.

Materials, devices, and returns

New devices (APs, cameras, switches, NVRs)

  1. Photo of the box label and serial number before you open it.
  2. Box, device, bracket, screws, and paperwork are one unit until the device is mounted, powered, and confirmed online.
  3. Not installing it today? Everything goes back in the box, tape with customer name, into the job tote on the staging shelf.
  4. Installed and tested? Flatten the box for the cardboard stack. Spare brackets and screws go in a labeled hardware bag (below).

Multi-day jobs: the job tote and the hardware bag

We cannot have a drawer for every SKU. We can have one tote per job and one bag per device. That is the whole system.

  • Job tote. Every active job gets one tote (any lidded bin), painter's tape on the end, customer name and date in Sharpie. Everything staged for that job that is not on a rack lives in it. It sits on the staging shelf between visits.
  • Hardware bag. The moment a device is out of its box and its hardware is loose, the hardware goes in a zip bag, Sharpie on the bag: customer – device model – what it is. Example: Beer Tree – U7 Pro – ceiling bracket + 4 screws. Bag goes in the job tote.
  • When the job closes, empty the tote: unused device hardware bags, still labeled, go in the drawer marked Device hardware – misc (sorted by manufacturer if there is more than one bag). Leftover consumables go back to their drawers. The tote gets wiped and re-taped for the next job.
  • No tote, no bags, no Sharpie? Text Kurtis and Rio, then use any lidded box and any bag you can label. Loose hardware on a shelf is not a solution.

Pulling stock and reporting low supplies

  1. Take what the job needs from the van. Open only what you will use.
  2. Any time you notice a drawer or rack item is at or below its minimum mark, or you take the last one, or you can see it will not cover the next job: photo of the drawer, text Kurtis and Rio LOW: [item] – about [how many] left. Same day, not later.
  3. Rio orders. You do not buy stock items unless Kurtis approves it for a job that cannot wait.
  4. If a drawer has no minimum mark, tell Kurtis and Rio what you think the minimum should be. You use it; you know.

Time and reporting

You do not start the next job until the last one is emailed. Park, write the email, send it, then drive. This is how your hours get billed and how you get paid. The email is to Kurtis, copy Rio.

What counts as time on a job

  • Start is when you leave the garage (or the previous customer) headed to that customer.
  • Stop is when you arrive back at the garage (or arrive at the next customer).
  • Travel to the site and travel back from it are part of that job's time. Record the drive minutes separately inside the block so they can be billed correctly.
  • Multiple jobs in one day: travel from job A to job B belongs to job B. Travel from the last job back to the garage belongs to the last job. Each job gets its own email.
  • Lunch and personal stops are not on any job. Note them so the math works.

Onsite work standards

You represent TCT the moment you pull into the lot. Clean clothes, no torn or offensive shirts, kind to every person you meet including the ones who are not the customer. Leave every job better than you found it: the work done right, the area cleaner than when you arrived, and the customer glad we came.

Before you touch a wall

  1. Read the job notes Kurtis sent. Know the device count, locations, and where the cables terminate.
  2. Walk the site with the customer contact. Confirm every device location and cable path out loud.
  3. Photo of existing conditions in every area you will work in (ceilings, closets, walls). This protects you.
  4. Locate the rack or termination point. Confirm there is power and space before you pull a single cable.

Pulling and terminating

  • Pull the category and rating (plenum or riser) the job notes call for. When the notes do not say, ask before you pull.
  • Support cable with J-hooks or Velcro at intervals. No staples, no resting on ceiling grid, no draping over light fixtures. Keep separation from power conductors; cross at 90 degrees when you must. (General low voltage installation practice.)
  • Service loop at each end. Enough to re-terminate twice.
  • Label both ends of every run before termination, using the TCT label printer. Same label on the patch panel port and the faceplate.
  • Test every run after termination with your tester. Photo of the result for each run.
  • Fire-stop any penetration through a rated wall or floor with fire caulk. When you are not sure whether a wall is rated, treat it as rated.

Mounting devices

  • Serial photo before mounting. Photo of the device mounted. Photo of the LED status once powered.
  • Cameras: confirm the view on a phone or laptop with the customer contact before you leave the ladder.
  • Access points: mount where the design says. If the design is impossible in the field, stop and text with a photo.

Your tools while you work

  • Tools live in your bag or on a drop cloth in one spot, not spread across the customer's floor, desks, or counters.
  • Ladder down and out of walkways when you step away. Nothing left in a hallway, doorway, or on a stair.
  • Pack up as you finish each area, not all at the end.

Do not, ever

  • Cut into live electrical, open a panel, or add a circuit. Licensed electrician only.
  • Say yes to extra work onsite. "Let me check with Kurtis" is the only answer.
  • Tell the customer about damage before Kurtis does. Damage is reported to Kurtis first, by call, and Kurtis decides what is said to the customer and by whom. You do not talk to the customer about damage without his approval.
  • Hide damage. Reported the same hour, it is a small bill. Hidden, it is a lost customer and your removal from the schedule.
  • Share passwords, Wi-Fi keys, or controller access with anyone. Kurtis only.
  • Work alone above 12 feet, or on a roof, or in a lift.

Closing out the job

  1. Every run tested, every test photographed.
  2. Every device online, every device photographed mounted and lit.
  3. Labels on both ends, photo of the patch panel and faceplates.
  4. Clean up: shop vac the drywall dust, collect every cut-off and package, wipe down the rack area. Trash and cardboard leave with you. Photo of the cleaned area.
  5. Walk the customer contact through what was done. Get a verbal thumbs-up.
  6. Sit in the van and send the job email before you drive anywhere.
  7. Return to the garage. Van reset. Interior photo texted. Keys and card to Kurtis.

Cardboard, packaging, and the dump run

Boxes we bring to a customer site often show pricing, model numbers, and serials. They do not stay at the site and they do not go in the customer's dumpster. They come back to the garage, flattened, and they leave the garage on a scheduled dump run.

Daily

  1. Flatten every box once its device is mounted and tested. Peel or black out any shipping label with pricing on it.
  2. Cardboard stacks flat in the van. Foam and plastic packaging goes in the trash bag.
  3. At the garage: cardboard to the cardboard corner, stacked flat. Trash bag to the garage trash cans.

The dump run: when and how

  • Trigger: the cardboard stack reaches the line marked on the wall, or the garage cans are full, or it has been two weeks since the last run, whichever comes first. The person who sees it hits the trigger texts Kurtis and Rio Dump run needed and, unless told otherwise, does the run that week.
  • Where: Broome County Landfill, 286 Knapp Road, Binghamton, NY 13902 (Town of Nanticoke). County landfill page.
  • Hours: Monday to Friday 7:00 AM to 2:45 PM, Saturday 7:00 AM to 11:45 AM. Closed Sundays and on Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's Day. Check the page before you go; hours change.
  • Load rules: the load must be covered or you are charged an automatic $20. Proof of Broome County residency or business is required at the gate. Bring the TCT Amex for any disposal fee.
  • At the landfill: ask the attendant where cardboard recycling goes versus general trash; follow their direction, not this page.
  • Afterward: receipt photo and the run's time go in a job email with the subject Job report – TCT dump run – [date]. Dump-run time is paid time.
  • Never leave TCT cardboard or packaging at a customer site, in a customer's dumpster, on a roadside, or in a residential can.
